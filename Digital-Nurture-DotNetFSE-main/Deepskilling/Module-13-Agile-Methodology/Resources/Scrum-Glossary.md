# Scrum & Agile Glossary

A reference dictionary of common terms used in Agile software development.

---

## Alphabetical Glossary

### A
* **Acceptance Criteria (AC)**: The specific functional and business requirements that a user story must satisfy to be accepted by the Product Owner.

### B
* **Backlog Refinement**: Ongoing meeting(s) where the Product Owner and Developers review, detail, estimate, and order items in the Product Backlog to make them ready for sprint planning. (Sometimes called *Backlog Grooming*).
* **Burndown Chart**: A daily chart showing the amount of work remaining in the sprint backlog.
* **Burnup Chart**: A chart tracking completed work against the total project scope over time, indicating scope changes.

### D
* **Definition of Done (DoD)**: A formal, global quality checklist (e.g., testing, peer review, styling) that all backlog items must meet to be considered a shippable product increment.
* **Definition of Ready (DoR)**: Guidelines determining if a backlog item is sufficiently understood, estimated, and small enough to be brought into an upcoming sprint.

### E
* **Epic**: A large requirement or feature that cannot fit into a single sprint and must be broken down into smaller user stories.

### F
* **Fibonacci Sequence**: A sequence of numbers (1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13...) used in relative estimation to represent increasing uncertainty as task sizes grow.

### I
* **Impediment**: Any block, hurdle, or obstacle that prevents developers from making progress on their sprint tasks (e.g., lack of credentials, team disputes, environment downtime).
* **Increment**: A concrete, functional step toward the Product Goal. It must meet the Definition of Done.

### P
* **Planning Poker**: A consensus-based estimation technique where developers use cards to relatively estimate the size of user stories.
* **Product Backlog**: An ordered list of everything required to improve or build the product. It is owned by the Product Owner.
* **Product Goal**: The long-term target or vision for the product.

### S
* **Spike**: A short, timeboxed research task to write prototype code, research an API, or investigate a technology, aimed at reducing technical risk or clarifying requirements.
* **Sprint**: A fixed-length, recurring iteration (1 to 4 weeks) during which the Scrum Team builds a shippable product increment.
* **Sprint Backlog**: The set of Product Backlog items selected for the current sprint, along with the plan to deliver them.
* **Sprint Goal**: The single, focused business objective for the current sprint.
* **Story Points**: An arbitrary unit of relative size, effort, complexity, and risk assigned to a user story.

### T
* **Theme**: A high-level strategic category or grouping of epics across a product line or business department.

### U
* **User Story**: A small requirement written from the perspective of an end-user, focusing on their goals and the business value.

### V
* **Velocity**: The average number of Story Points a team completes per sprint.

### W
* **WIP Limit (Work in Progress Limit)**: A rule restricting the maximum number of items that can be in a specific status (e.g., "In Progress" or "In Review") simultaneously, to improve workflow efficiency and highlight bottlenecks.
