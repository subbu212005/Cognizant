# The Four Core Values of Agile

The four core values of the Agile Manifesto serve as the ultimate decision-making framework for agile practitioners. When processes conflict with progress, these values guide the team.

---

## 1. Individuals and Interactions over Processes and Tools

> *"People are primary; processes and tools are secondary. The best tools in the world cannot compensate for a lack of communication or team collaboration."*

### Breakdown
* **Processes & Tools (Right)**: Issue trackers (Jira, Trello), IDEs, Slack, and formal workflow protocols are highly valuable. They keep work organized.
* **Individuals & Interactions (Left)**: Face-to-face (or synchronous) conversations, trust, collaboration, and psychological safety are far more critical. 

### Real-World Interpretation
* **The Agile Way**: If a developer has a question about a feature, they talk directly with the Product Owner instead of filing a Jira ticket and waiting days for a comment.
* **The Anti-Pattern**: A team refuses to collaborate on an urgent bug fix because "the ticketing system requires a specific approval state first."

---

## 2. Working Software over Comprehensive Documentation

> *"Working code is the only true measure of progress. Documents cannot be run by users, nor do they generate business value."*

### Breakdown
* **Comprehensive Documentation (Right)**: Technical designs, API specs, architectural diagrams, and user manuals are important for maintenance and onboarding.
* **Working Software (Left)**: An application that actually runs, fulfills user needs, and can be deployed to production.

### Real-World Interpretation
* **The Agile Way**: The team builds a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) with a clean, self-documenting codebase and automated tests, releasing it to users to get feedback.
* **The Anti-Pattern**: A team spends six months writing a 200-page System Architecture Document before writing a single line of code, only to find the tech stack they selected is outdated.

---

## 3. Customer Collaboration over Contract Negotiation

> *"We treat our customers as partners, not adversaries. Collaborative relationships lead to better products than rigid legal contracts."*

### Breakdown
* **Contract Negotiation (Right)**: Service Level Agreements (SLAs), Statements of Work (SOWs), and formal legal agreements establish boundaries and protect businesses.
* **Customer Collaboration (Left)**: Active, ongoing discussions with customers/users to understand their pain points and co-create solutions.

### Real-World Interpretation
* **The Agile Way**: The Product Owner meets with customer representatives weekly to showcase progress and pivot features based on what the customers actually like.
* **The Anti-Pattern**: A developer realizes a feature requested in the contract will harm the user experience, but builds it anyway because "it's in the contract, and changing it requires a legal addendum."

---

## 4. Responding to Change over Following a Plan

> *"The world changes rapidly. The ability to pivot is our greatest competitive advantage. A plan is a hypothesis, not a promise."*

### Breakdown
* **Following a Plan (Right)**: Milestones, budgets, and release roadmaps help set expectations and align business goals.
* **Responding to Change (Left)**: The flexibility to scrap outdated plans and adapt to new information, market shifts, or technology discoveries.

### Real-World Interpretation
* **The Agile Way**: A competitor releases a new feature that threatens your product's market share. You immediately adjust your backlog to address this threat in the next sprint, pausing previous roadmap items.
* **The Anti-Pattern**: A team continues building a desktop-only application for a market that has shifted entirely to mobile, just because the Gantt chart says "Desktop Phase is Scheduled through Q4."
