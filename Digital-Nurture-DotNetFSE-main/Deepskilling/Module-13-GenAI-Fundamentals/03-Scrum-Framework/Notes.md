# Notes: Scrum Theory and Core Values

Scrum is not a methodology; it is a framework containing a set of roles, events, and artifacts. Scrum is built on **empiricism** and **lean thinking**.

---

## The Three Pillars of Empiricism

Empirical process control asserts that knowledge comes from experience and making decisions based on what is observed. Scrum employs an iterative, incremental approach to optimize predictability and control risk.

Scrum is built on three pillars:

### 1. Transparency
The emergent process and work must be visible to those performing the work as well as those receiving the work.
* *In Practice*: Backlogs must be public, blockers must be raised immediately, and the true status of work must be visible on the board.

### 2. Inspection
Scrum artifacts and progress toward agreed-upon goals must be inspected frequently and diligently to detect potential variances or problems.
* *In Practice*: Daily Scrums inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal. Sprint Reviews inspect the Increment against the Product Goal.

### 3. Adaptation
If any aspects of a process deviate outside acceptable limits, or if the resulting product is unacceptable, the process being applied or the materials being produced must be adjusted as soon as possible.
* *In Practice*: The Sprint Retrospective is the dedicated ceremony for inspecting the team's processes and adapting them.

## The Five Scrum Values

Successful use of Scrum depends on people becoming more proficient in living five core values. These values guide the team's decisions and behaviors:

```
     Commitment     Courage      Focus
             \         |         /
              \        |        /
              Openness---Respect
```

### 1. Commitment
The Scrum Team commits to achieving its goals and supporting each other.
* *Behavior*: Taking ownership of sprint backlog items and striving to deliver the Sprint Goal together, without throwing work "over the wall" to QA.

### 2. Courage
Scrum Team members have the courage to do the right thing and work on tough problems.
* *Behavior*: Speaking up when a deadline is unrealistic, admitting when a design approach failed, or pointing out technical debt that needs fixing.

### 3. Focus
Everyone focuses on the work of the Sprint and the goals of the Scrum Team to make the best possible progress.
* *Behavior*: Minimizing multitasking, refusing to pull in unplanned work mid-sprint, and dedicating time to code reviews and pairing to get stories done.

### 4. Openness
The Scrum Team and its stakeholders agree to be open about all the work and the challenges with performing the work.
* *Behavior*: Raising impediments early, being transparent about bugs, and receiving constructive feedback during reviews and retrospectives.

### 5. Respect
Scrum Team members respect each other to be capable, independent people.
* *Behavior*: Valuing diverse technical opinions, giving credit to colleagues, and treating product owners and business stakeholders as peers.
